# My Story

I grew up fascinated by how software could solve real, messy human problems. That curiosity took me from a computer science classroom at Cornell all the way to Y Combinator, and eventually to building documentation infrastructure used by some of the most influential developer teams in the world. This page is the fuller version of that journey — the detours, experiments, and moments that shaped how I think about building products and companies.

## The Journey

<Steps>
  <Step title="Cornell University — Information Science">
    I studied Information Science at Cornell University, which sits at the intersection of computer science, design, and social systems. It was the right fit for someone who cared about *how* technology is used, not just how it's built. Cornell gave me a strong technical foundation and, more importantly, the habit of asking "why does this actually matter to people?" — a question I still ask every day at Mintlify.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Meta (Facebook) — Software Engineering Intern">
    During my time at Cornell, I interned at Meta as a software engineering intern. Working inside one of the largest engineering organizations in the world was eye-opening. I got to see how massive systems are built, how teams collaborate at scale, and — honestly — where big-company bureaucracy slows things down. The experience made me more confident that I wanted to build something from scratch rather than be one node in a very large graph.
  </Step>

  <Step title="pe•ple & Bettermode — Startup + Staff Engineer">
    Before Mintlify, I worked on an early-stage startup in the community-building space that was eventually acquired by Bettermode. I stayed on as a staff engineer at Bettermode, helping build out the platform post-acquisition. Working across the full lifecycle of a startup — early-stage chaos, product-market fit search, and eventual acquisition — taught me more about company building than any textbook could. It also put me squarely in the world of developer tools and product infrastructure, which set the stage for everything that came next.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Bain Capital Ventures — Investor">
    I spent time on the investment team at Bain Capital Ventures, where I got a front-row seat to how VCs evaluate companies and markets. Looking at dozens of startups across developer tools, infrastructure, and SaaS sharpened my pattern recognition for what makes a company compelling — and what makes a market genuinely large. It also helped me understand how investors think, which has been invaluable as a founder on the other side of the table.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Founding Mintlify — Senior Year at Cornell (YC W22)">
    My co-founder and I started Mintlify during my senior year at Cornell. The original insight was painfully simple: writing documentation was tedious, and most docs were bad as a result. We wanted to make it so fast and pleasant to write great docs that there was no excuse not to. We applied to Y Combinator, got in for the Winter 2022 batch, and spent the semester splitting time between finishing our degrees and building the earliest version of Mintlify. We launched, got our first customers, and haven't looked back since.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Scaling to 20,000+ Teams">
    After YC, we moved to San Francisco and went heads-down on growth. The traction was real — developer teams genuinely loved what we were building, and word spread fast. Within a couple of years, Mintlify was powering documentation for over 20,000 teams, including Anthropic, Microsoft, and Amazon. What started as "a nicer way to write docs" had grown into core infrastructure for some of the most important products in tech. We're still early, and the roadmap ahead is the most exciting it's ever been.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Beyond Work

I try to maintain a life outside of company-building, though I'll admit the line can blur when you genuinely love what you're working on. A few things about me that don't show up on a resume:

I think a lot about the long arc of how humans share knowledge — from books to the internet to AI systems — and where we are in that story. It's part of why Mintlify feels like more than a productivity tool to me; it's part of something bigger.

I'm an avid learner in the most chaotic possible sense: I'll go deep on a topic for a few weeks, emerge with strong opinions, and then get pulled in a completely different direction. Right now that topic is how large language models reason about structured knowledge, but ask me again in a month.

San Francisco has been a great home base. The density of people building interesting things is unlike anywhere else, and I've found that some of the best ideas come from casual conversations over coffee or a walk through the Mission. If you're in the city, I'm always happy to meet.
