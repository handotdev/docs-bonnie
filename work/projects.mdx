## Project Deep Dives

<Accordion title="Mintlify — Documentation for the Modern Web">
  Documentation has always been one of those things that every team knows matters and almost no team does well. Before Mintlify, the options were clunky wikis, hand-rolled static sites, or expensive enterprise platforms that felt like they were designed in 2008. Han and his co-founder set out to build something that developers would actually enjoy using — both to write and to read.

  The result is a platform that takes a docs-as-code approach: write in MDX, push to GitHub, and get a fast, beautiful, fully-featured documentation site with search, versioning, API references, and AI-powered features baked in. Mintlify handles the infrastructure so teams can focus on the content.

  The company was founded in October 2022 as part of Y Combinator's W22 cohort and has grown to serve 20,000+ teams worldwide. It has raised $66.8M across four funding rounds and operates from San Francisco with a team of 60–70 people. Customers range from early-stage startups publishing their first API reference to large public companies managing complex, multi-product documentation.

  As AI becomes an increasingly important consumer of documentation — not just human readers — Mintlify has been at the forefront of thinking about what it means to write docs for both audiences at once.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="pe•ple — Community Infrastructure for Startups">
  In late 2020, Han co-founded pe•ple with a clear hypothesis: early-stage startup teams needed a dedicated place to build community that wasn't Slack, wasn't Discord, and wasn't a generic forum. pe•ple was that place — a platform designed for the specific rhythms and needs of startup ecosystems, where signal matters and noise kills momentum.

  The product helped startup founders create branded community spaces for their users, customers, and fellow builders. It was built fast, launched quickly, and found real traction among the people it was designed for.

  In February 2021 — roughly four months after founding — pe•ple was acquired by Bettermode, a larger community platform company. Han stayed on as a Staff Software Engineer through May 2021, helping Bettermode apply pe•ple's learnings to their enterprise and growth-stage customer base. A short lifecycle, but a meaningful one: pe•ple proved that a small, focused team could build a product worth acquiring in under half a year.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="FoodFul — Smarter Dairy Farming Through Data">
  FoodFul was Han's first company and, in some ways, his most unconventional one. The premise: dairy farmers were managing large herds with surprisingly little real-time data about individual animal health, feeding patterns, and productivity. FoodFul built a cloud-connected monitoring system that used sensors to track cows and surface actionable insights to farm operators.

  The company raised $150K in 2019, giving Han his first experience with fundraising, hardware supply chains, customer discovery in a decidedly non-tech industry, and the unglamorous work of making software useful for people who have no patience for software that doesn't work. It also earned Han recognition as an SVB Fellow in January 2020.

  FoodFul didn't become a unicorn, but it became something arguably more valuable: a formative experience that shaped how Han thinks about real-world problems, non-obvious customer segments, and the gap between a clever technical solution and one that actually fits into someone's life.
</Accordion>

### WeAreDevelopers World Congress 2026 — North America

**September 24, 2026**

Han's talk explored one of the most significant shifts happening in software right now: the way software is consumed is changing, and documentation is at the center of that change.

For most of the history of software, documentation was written for humans — developers reading a reference page, scanning an integration guide, or searching for an error message at 11pm. That assumption is quietly being overturned. AI agents are increasingly the primary readers of documentation, consuming it programmatically to understand APIs, generate code, answer user questions, and execute tasks autonomously.

The talk examined what that shift means for how teams should think about writing, structuring, and maintaining their docs — and why the teams that get this right early will have a meaningful advantage as AI-native workflows become the default. It's a conversation Han is uniquely positioned to lead: Mintlify sits at the intersection of developer documentation and AI, and he's watched this transformation happen in real time across thousands of teams.
